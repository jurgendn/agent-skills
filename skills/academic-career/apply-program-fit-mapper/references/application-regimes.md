# Application regimes — what to shortlist, and what "a target" even is

Before shortlisting, establish **how each target actually admits people**. The regime decides what the unit of a shortlist entry is, whether outreach is on the critical path, and how many targets are realistic.

**Country selects which institutional rules to investigate. Country does not determine strategy by itself** — the same country routinely contains several regimes, and a single university can use different ones per department or funding source. Verify per target.

---

## The four regimes

### Supervisor-first — the unit is *a person and a project*

A named supervisor and an agreed topic precede the formal application or registration.

- **France:** a doctoral candidate needs a thesis topic and thesis supervisor before enrolment, with acceptance by a doctoral school; the work sits within a laboratory.
- **French-speaking Belgium:** official Wallonia-Brussels guidance states you must have a **thesis proposal and the written agreement of a supervisor before you can register**. Each institution sets its own admission criteria on top of that.

**Consequences for shortlisting:** outreach is the critical path, not a courtesy — route these to `apply-cold-email-drafter` early. Depth beats breadth: two or three well-researched targets outperform ten. A shortlist entry without a named person is not yet a shortlist entry. Expect long latency, since the pipeline depends on an individual replying.

### Vacancy-first — the unit is *an advertised position*

The position is a job and the successful applicant is an employee.

- **Sweden:** an admitted doctoral candidate is both a doctoral student and a university employee — salary, paid holiday, pension. Positions are posted alongside other jobs and applied to individually, **not** through the national portal used for bachelor's and master's admission.
- **Norway:** PhD candidates are not considered students but employees; positions are announced as vacancies.
- Also covers MSCA Doctoral Networks and most project-funded positions advertised via EURAXESS, wherever they are located.

**Consequences for shortlisting:** you cannot shortlist a *department* here — you shortlist open positions, and the list is only valid while they are open. Monitoring vacancy boards is the actual work. Fit is assessed against the advertised project's stated requirements, so a target is worth listing only if the requirement-to-evidence mapping is genuinely strong. Speculative cold-email to a professor should not displace applying to their advertised vacancy through the formal process.

### Call / competition-first — the unit is *a specific call*

Admission runs through a formal published call with ranked selection.

- **Italy:** doctoral recruitment is commonly organised through *bandi di concorso* with ranked selection, sometimes assessing a research proposal and then interviewing shortlisted candidates.

**Consequences for shortlisting:** the call, not the university, is the entry — two calls at one university can have different criteria, topics, and deadlines. Calls open and close on fixed cycles, so the shortlist is deadline-structured. Contacting an academic informally may still help, but it does not substitute for the formal submission.

### Department-first — the unit is *a department with several plausible advisors*

The applicant is admitted to a department or cohort, may rotate, and settles with an advisor later. Typical of US PhD admissions.

**Consequences for shortlisting:** list several plausible advisors per school, since no single one is decisive. Pre-application contact is optional and sometimes explicitly discouraged — check the department's own guidance rather than assuming outreach helps. Breadth is more viable here than in the supervisor-first family.

## Funding is a separate axis

Identify the funding regime independently of the admission regime, because they have **different selectors** and may require different documents:

- funding attached to the **programme** (admission and money decided together);
- a **separate scholarship** with its own criteria, deadline, and sometimes its own committee;
- funding attached to a **specific project** or an **employment contract**;
- **nominated** schemes, where the host institution or a national sending partner screens and nominates before the funder ever sees the application.

Where two selectors exist, one document rarely satisfies both. Note which reads which.

Two failure modes worth checking for explicitly: a country may have **no general government scholarship** at a given level, in which case there is nothing to apply to and the search should move to programme-specific or project funding; and some support is **eligibility-assessed rather than merit-assessed** — Italian regional DSU grants are income-based (ISEE) against academic requirements, so they belong on a checklist, not a shortlist of competitive targets.

---

## Mismatch costs

- Department-first strategy applied to a supervisor-first target → an application with no advocate.
- Supervisor-first outreach applied to a department-first target → reads as presumptuous, and can violate stated policy.
- Treating a vacancy-first target as an open-ended admission → applying to a department that does not admit that way, or missing that the position closed.
- Treating a call-first target as a rolling application → missing the only window in the cycle.

---

## Volatility

Deadlines, call cycles, vacancy availability, nomination quotas, and eligibility rules change every cycle, and institutions vary within every country named here. Everything above describes **published structures**, not national temperament. Verify each target against its own official pages before committing it to a shortlist.
