# CV format conventions and required documents

Academic CV *content* — what counts as research evidence, how to frame contribution — does not vary much across systems. **Format and required fields do**, and a CV rejected on format never gets read for content.

## The governing rule

**The call decides.** Before advising on format, establish what the specific application actually requires:

1. The call's or programme page's **required-documents list**.
2. Whether a **mandatory template or format** is named (this is common and non-negotiable where it appears).
3. Whether the institution publishes its **own CV template** — if so, use it, whatever the general convention says.
4. Any stated **page limit**.

Where the call is silent, default to a standard academic CV: reverse-chronological, no photo, no personal data beyond name and contact details, no page limit anxiety (academic CVs are allowed to be long; industry résumé compression is not the goal).

Do not advise from national convention when the call is available. Ask the user for it, or tell them exactly where to look.

## Mandatory templates — check before writing anything

Some processes reject non-conforming documents without review. Known cases to check for:

- **Scholarship programmes with their own forms.** Several require their CV and reference letters on official templates and disqualify other formats automatically. If the user is applying to a scholarship, confirm this before drafting.
- **Europass.** Not the EU-wide standard it is often assumed to be, but genuinely required or strongly preferred in specific places: **EU-funded research calls** (Horizon Europe, ERC, MSCA) and **Italian public-sector and university calls** — an Italian *bando* asking for *formato europeo* means Europass. Outside those, a modern academic CV is generally the better document.
- **University-specific application portals** frequently define the CV's expected sections. German applications routed through **uni-assist**, for instance, expect a tabular, chronological CV covering personal data, education, practical experience, and additional qualifications.

## Photo, date of birth, and personal data

This is the field most often gotten wrong, in both directions.

**Do not carry industry-résumé conventions into an academic application.** The German *Lebenslauf* of the job market — photo top-right, date and place of birth, sometimes marital status — is a **hiring-market document**, not the CV a university or a research funder expects. Germany's General Equal Treatment Act (AGG) means these cannot be *required*, and university-application CVs are typically simpler than job-market ones.

**The rule:** include personal data fields **when the call or template asks for them**, and omit them when it does not. Where the user is unsure, omitting a photo from an academic CV is the safer default and is not read as an error.

Do not tell a user that a given country "expects a photo." That claim varies by document type, sector, institution, and year, and asserting it confidently can put discriminatory personal data into an application that never asked for it.

## Publication and output listing

- Use the **citation style of the target field**, consistently, and state author order as published — do not reorder to move the user forward.
- Mark status honestly and distinctly: published / accepted / under review / in preparation. Conflating these is a serious integrity problem, not a formatting choice, and reviewers in small fields do check.
- Preprints are listed as preprints. Where the field treats arXiv posting as a normal step (ML, physics, maths), this is a strength; where it does not, it needs no apology either.
- Where the user has no publications, do not manufacture a Publications section. Research experience, technical outputs, and software or datasets carry the signal instead.

## Degree, grade, and transcript representation

- Give the degree its **actual awarded name**, not a translated approximation, and add a gloss where the name will not be recognised.
- Report the grade **on its native scale** with the scale stated (`3.6/4.0`, `1.3 (German scale, 1.0 best)`, `GPA 8.2/10`). Never silently convert to a foreign scale — the receiving institution converts, and a self-converted figure that disagrees with their conversion looks like inflation.
- Where a degree's structure will not be obvious (a 4-year bachelor's against a system expecting 3+2, an integrated master's), state the duration and whether a thesis was involved. This is also an **eligibility** question — route it to `apply-dossier-evaluator` where the answer determines admissibility.

## Language

Write in the language the call specifies. Where a call permits either, English is normally the safer choice for research applications. If the user submits in a second language, the CV should be proofread by a fluent speaker — a formatting error is forgivable and a garbled degree title is not.

---

## Volatility

Required-document lists, mandatory templates, page limits, Europass requirements, and personal-data expectations are **set per call and change between cycles**. Everything above is the default for when the call is silent. The call, the template, and the institution's own instructions always win — say so to the user rather than asserting a national rule.
