# Statement conventions and selection regimes

How to adapt a statement across systems without inventing a persona or a stereotype.

## The model

**Applicant Evidence + Target Evidence + Selection/Funding Regime → Narrative Strategy**

- **Applicant Evidence** — what the user has actually done. Fixed.
- **Target Evidence** — this university, department, laboratory, supervisor.
- **Selection / Funding Regime** — the *published* objectives, selection criteria, application architecture, and hard constraints of the specific programme or funder.

The third term is deliberately **not** "regional cultural prior." A funder's published criteria are observable and checkable; a nation's character is neither. Country enters only where it produces a documented institutional mechanism. Where no such mechanism exists, country is not an input.

## The headline rule

**Country selects which institutional rules to investigate. Country does not determine the writing style.**

## The invariant

**Facts fixed; weights vary.**

Adapting a statement changes **which evidence is salient, in what order, at what depth, and framed against which criterion**. It never changes what the applicant did, never adds a claim, and never subtracts an inconvenient one.

---

## Three anti-patterns — treat as hard rules

**1. Cultural-praise anti-pattern.** Never demonstrate fit by praising generalised national characteristics.

> ✗ "I deeply admire Germany's culture of precision and innovation."
> ✗ "Singapore is internationally renowned for excellence."
> ✗ "China's long history inspires me."

These provide zero evidence of applicant–programme fit, and they signal that the applicant researched the *country* instead of the *lab*. Institutional context may change which existing evidence is emphasised; it must never become an object of generic praise. If a sentence would survive being moved to an application to a different country, it is doing no work.

**2. Identity-mutation anti-pattern.** Do not invent a different applicant for each target. Rotate the same object: preserve claims and evidence, change salience, ordering, framing, and level of detail according to *verified* target criteria. Seven targets do not mean seven identities.

**3. Force-fit anti-pattern.** Where a programme declares a theme, specialisation, or track — "digital and green transition", "AI for health", a mission-scoped doctoral school, a topic-restricted call — never manufacture a connection to it by relabelling unrelated work.

> ✗ "My work on matrix factorisation is fundamentally about enabling the green transition."
> ✗ "I have always been passionate about sustainability" (attached to a CV showing none).
> ✓ "I built the demand-forecasting model for [X]'s distribution network, which is the load-balancing problem the Energy Systems pathway studies at grid scale."

The test is the **swap test**: substitute a different specialisation into the sentence. If it still reads fine, the sentence is filler and proves nothing — the same diagnostic as the cultural-praise rule. A real bridge names specific work the applicant actually did and the specific part of the declared theme it serves, and it breaks when the theme changes.

Facts stay fixed here too. A theme decides which real work leads and how deeply it is described; it does not license inventing an interest, promising a pivot the CV cannot support, or restating a project in the programme's vocabulary and calling that fit. Where **no honest bridge exists, the correct output is that this is the wrong programme** — not a better-worded paragraph. Route thematic screening to `apply-program-fit-mapper` before drafting; if it returned *stretch* or *outside scope*, say so rather than writing around it.

## The geopolitical inference gate

A true fact about a country's strategy **does not by itself license** an emphasis in a statement. It licenses one only when the full chain is documented:

**geopolitical objective → funding/programme objective → published selection criterion**

This chain is falsifiable, which is the point. **Australia Awards passes it** — the scheme's own policy documents tie education to partner-country development, leadership, and return-home contribution, and that is a stated objective. **"China emphasises technological self-reliance, therefore mention strategic self-reliance in your SOP"** fails it: the first half may be true, but no published selection criterion connects it to how an individual applicant is scored. Do not make that leap, and do not let a user make it — the advice can actively damage an application depending on reader, field, and year.

## The extraction method

Run this for **each target separately**. It is the reusable part of this file; everything below it is illustration.

1. **Identify the application regime** — how does one actually get admitted here? (See the three families below.)
2. **Identify the funding regime** — is the money attached to the programme, to a separate scholarship, to a specific project, or to an employment contract? These have different selectors.
3. **Extract the published criteria** — from the call, the scheme's own pages, the vacancy text. Write them down as a list. **A declared theme, specialisation, or track scope belongs on that list**: where the programme states one, relevance to it is a criterion the statement must answer, and the answer must be a real bridge (anti-pattern 3).
4. **Reweight the applicant's existing evidence against that list.** Every criterion should have identifiable support somewhere in the package.
5. **Note the hard constraints** — eligibility, residency, return-home conditions, mandatory templates, deadlines.

**Two selectors may apply to one application.** A scholarship may be nominated by the host institution, or routed through a national sending partner, before the funder ever sees it. Where that happens, one document rarely satisfies both selectors equally — establish which one reads which document.

## Scholarship genres

The genre, not the country, tells you what evidence should lead. A scheme's genre is read off its **published objectives**, and two schemes in the same country routinely belong to different genres.

| Genre | What must dominate the application |
|---|---|
| **Academic excellence** | Academic record, research potential, coherent study/research project. Do not distort into a leadership story merely because funding is attached. |
| **Development / change** | A problem encountered professionally → the missing expertise → the programme → return and application → concrete institutional or societal change. Usually requires documented work experience. |
| **Leadership / influence** | Leadership track record, influence, networks, and a specific future trajectory. A pure research statement is the wrong genre here. |
| **Industry / professional** | Technical competence and the ability to translate advanced study into practice. Deployed systems and engineering work become unusually strong evidence. |
| **Research cooperation** | Research proposal, named host and supervisor, methodological feasibility, and the prospect of continuing collaboration. |
| **Consortium / mobility** | Why *this multi-institution structure* is necessary for the trajectory — what the combination and mobility solve that one ordinary programme would not. |
| **Diplomatic / bilateral** | Merit plus a coherent study or research plan fitting the scheme's stated cooperation objectives. Never "I love this country's culture." |

**Genre is not inferable from the funder's nationality.** A single national agency can run an academic-excellence scheme and a development-change scheme side by side, with different eligibility and different selectors.

## The three PhD application regimes

Which family a target belongs to determines the unit of customisation. Verify per target — countries cross these boundaries depending on the position and the funding source.

**Supervisor-first.** A named supervisor and an agreed topic precede formal application or registration. France's doctoral structure requires a thesis topic and supervisor before enrolment, with doctoral-school acceptance, and doctoral work sits within a laboratory. French-speaking Belgium is more explicit still: official Wallonia-Brussels guidance states you must have a **thesis proposal and the written agreement of a supervisor before you can register**, though each institution sets its own admission criteria on top. Here supervisor correspondence is the critical path, and the statement's centre of gravity is *research problem + laboratory + supervisor*.

**Vacancy-first.** The position is an advertised job and the successful applicant is an employee. In Sweden an admitted doctoral candidate is both a doctoral student and a university employee, with salary, paid holiday and pension; positions are posted alongside other jobs and applied to individually — **not** through the national portal used for bachelor's and master's admission. Norway is structurally the same: PhD candidates are not considered students but employees, with positions announced as vacancies. **The application behaves like a technical job application** — map each stated requirement to specific evidence and address the advertised project directly. Generic "changemaker" framing consumes space that requirement-matching should occupy, unless the vacancy asks for it.

**Call / competition-first.** Admission runs through a formal published call with ranked selection. Italian doctoral recruitment is commonly organised through *bandi di concorso* with ranked selection, sometimes assessing a research proposal and then interviewing shortlisted candidates. The unit of customisation is **the exact call** and its stated criteria, not the country.

Cold-emailing is the primary pathway in the supervisor-first family, is secondary to the formal process in the call-first family, and should not displace a formal recruitment process in the vacancy-first family.

## Other verified regime differences

**Australia — Awards and ordinary RTP are different genres.** Australia Awards is a development-policy instrument targeting partner-country development, leadership and lasting change, and contribution after returning home. Scholars must return home on completion and **remain outside Australia for at least two years**; seeking to remain in or return to Australia inside that window incurs a debt for the full cost of the scholarship, which for a doctorate is substantial. This is a hard constraint with real financial consequences and must be surfaced, not treated as essay framing. Ordinary **RTP** scholarships are administered by individual universities with their own selection processes, centred on research readiness, a supervisor, and a feasible project.

**Hong Kong — HKPFS publishes its rubric.** The scheme names four yardsticks: academic excellence (stated as the prime consideration), research ability/potential, communication and interpersonal skills, and leadership abilities. Shortlisted applicants are assessed by one of two panels. A published four-part rubric lets you allocate evidence directly — note that communication and leadership are scored here in a way most departmental PhD applications do not score them.

**Named-project vs department-first.** Where the application targets a specific funded project or advertised position, argue fit to *that project* and show its description was read. Where it targets a department or cohort (typical US admissions), argue research direction and fit to several plausible advisors, since the advisor is not yet fixed.

**Application architecture varies independently of everything else.** A separate research proposal may carry the research content; where required, the statement should not duplicate it — Tsinghua, for example, requires international PhD applicants to submit research experience or a research plan *in addition to* the personal statement. Some European calls expect an actual **letter** — salutation, addressed to a named committee, signed — rather than a titled essay. **Fellowship-specific criteria are not national criteria:** NSF's GRFP requires both statements to address Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts under explicit headings, but that is a GRFP requirement, not evidence about US admissions generally, and eligibility is restricted to U.S. citizens, nationals, and permanent residents.

**Some funding has no narrative at all.** Italian regional DSU support is income-assessed (ISEE) against academic requirements — eligibility, not motivation. And where a country has no general government scholarship for a level of study, do not invent a narrative to optimise for one; identify the actual funding mechanism instead.

**Master's-level statements.** Programme and curriculum choice, the bridge from prior study, and post-degree plans carry the document. Do not push an applicant to invent a research proposal the call did not ask for.

## Worked contrasts: the same CV, different applications, no fact changed

This is what "rotate the same object" means operationally. Take one applicant with an industry research project, a publication, and some engineering work.

**DAAD study/research grants vs DAAD EPOS — same funder, different genre.** The main DAAD scholarship lines weight academic qualification, the quality and feasibility of the study or research plan, its integration into the applicant's academic trajectory, motivation, and prospects. **EPOS** is a development instrument: it requires at least **two years of relevant professional experience** and explicitly assesses development-related motivation, social responsibility, and the capacity to initiate and support processes of change. The same industry project becomes *evidence of technical preparation* for the first and *a problem encountered in practice that motivates the training* for the second. Nothing about the project changes.

**Master Mind vs VLIR-UOS — same region, different genre.** Both fund study in Flanders. One is an academic-excellence scheme routed through host-institution nomination; the other is a development scheme where development relevance and the capacity to become an agent of change are explicit selection content. The same applicant leads with academic record for one, and with professional problem plus intended change for the other.

**Swedish PhD vacancy vs SI scholarship — same country, opposite documents.** A Swedish PhD position is an advertised job: match stated requirements to evidence and address the project. The Swedish Institute scholarship for global professionals requires documented work and leadership experience and frames selection around contribution to sustainable development. Leadership content is *wasted space* in the first and *core selection evidence* in the second.

In every case the facts are identical. Only salience, ordering, depth, and the criterion each item is framed against change.

## Register claims are weakly sourced — do not assert them

Claims about how *personal*, *narrative*, or *formal* a statement should be by region are **widely repeated folklore**, and sources contradict each other on direction. They are hypotheses to test against the prompt, never rules to state to a user.

Reliable substitute: **read the prompt's own verbs.** "Why are you motivated to apply for this position" asks a different question from "describe your research background and goals." Answer the question actually asked, in the length actually allowed.

## Self-presentation and credit

Applicants most often understate their own contribution — "we developed," "I was involved in" — for work they personally did. This is a **precision** problem before it is a cultural one: a reader cannot award credit the document does not attribute.

Calibrate toward accuracy in both directions. Never inflate; never leave real work unattributed. Where stating a contribution feels uncomfortable, ask what specifically the user did — the discomfort usually locates the exact sentence, and the fix is factual specificity, not louder language. Do not coach a persona, and do not make claims about what people from any country are like.

## Language

Write in the language the call specifies. A statement in a second language should be read by a fluent speaker — committees distinguish awkward phrasing (forgivable) from an argument they cannot follow (not).

---

## Volatility

**Every scheme-specific number changes per cycle** — GPA cutoffs, nomination quotas, years-of-experience minimums, word limits, deadlines, stipend amounts, eligible-country lists, and residency conditions. This file deliberately encodes **genres, regimes, and the extraction method** rather than a scholarship catalogue, because a catalogue rots and the mechanisms do not. Get every specific from the live official call, and say so to the user rather than asserting it.
