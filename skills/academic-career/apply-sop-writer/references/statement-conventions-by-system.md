# Statement conventions and selection regimes

How to adapt a statement across systems without inventing a persona or a stereotype.

## The model

\[
\textbf{Applicant Evidence} + \textbf{Target Evidence} + \textbf{Selection / Funding Regime} \rightarrow \textbf{Narrative Strategy}
\]

- **Applicant Evidence** — what the user has actually done. Fixed.
- **Target Evidence** — this university, department, laboratory, supervisor.
- **Selection / Funding Regime** — the *published* objectives, selection criteria, application architecture, and hard constraints of the specific programme or funder.

The third term is deliberately **not** "regional cultural prior." A funder's published criteria are observable and checkable; a nation's character is neither. Country enters only where it produces a documented institutional mechanism — France's doctoral structure, Australia Awards' development mandate, HKPFS's published rubric. Where no such mechanism exists, country is not an input.

## The invariant

\[
\boxed{\textbf{facts fixed; weights vary}}
\]

Adapting a statement changes **which evidence is salient, in what order, at what depth, and framed against which criterion**. It never changes what the applicant did, never adds a claim, and never subtracts an inconvenient one.

---

## Two anti-patterns — treat as hard rules

**1. Cultural-praise anti-pattern.** Never demonstrate fit by praising generalised national characteristics.

> ✗ "I deeply admire Germany's culture of precision and innovation."
> ✗ "Singapore is internationally renowned for excellence."
> ✗ "China's long history inspires me."

These provide zero evidence of applicant–programme fit, and they signal that the applicant researched the *country* instead of the *lab*. Institutional context may change which existing evidence is emphasised; it must never become an object of generic praise. If a sentence would survive being moved to an application to a different country, it is doing no work.

**2. Identity-mutation anti-pattern.** Do not invent a different applicant for each target. Rotate the same object: preserve claims and evidence, change salience, ordering, framing, and level of detail according to *verified* target criteria. Seven targets do not mean seven identities.

## The geopolitical inference gate

A true fact about a country's strategy does **not** license an emphasis in a statement:

\[
\text{geopolitical fact} \nRightarrow \text{SOP emphasis}
\]

It licenses one only when the full chain is documented:

\[
\text{geopolitical objective} \rightarrow \text{funding / programme objective} \rightarrow \text{published selection criterion}
\]

This chain is falsifiable, which is the point. **Australia Awards passes it** — the scheme's own policy documents tie education to partner-country development, leadership, and return-home contribution, and that is a stated objective. **"China emphasises technological self-reliance, therefore mention strategic self-reliance in your SOP"** fails it: the first half may be true, but no published selection criterion connects it to how an individual applicant is scored. Do not make that leap, and do not let a user make it — the advice can actively damage an application depending on reader, field, and year.

## Verified regime differences

Each of these is an institutional mechanism, not a national temperament. Verify against the live call before relying on any of them.

### France — doctoral structure is supervisor- and laboratory-bound

Campus France states that a doctoral candidate needs a **thesis topic and a thesis supervisor before enrolment**, with acceptance by a doctoral school; doctoral work is conducted within a laboratory.

**Do not universalise this into "contact a supervisor before submitting any application."** Procedures differ by institution and by funding route. What it does license: for a French doctorate, the statement's centre of gravity is *research problem + laboratoire + directeur de thèse*, not autobiography.

### Australia — Awards and ordinary RTP are different genres

- **Australia Awards** is a development-policy instrument. It targets partner-country development, leadership and lasting change, and contribution after returning home. Scholars must return home on completion and **remain outside Australia for at least two years**; seeking permanent residence inside that window incurs a debt for the full scholarship cost (which for a PhD can reach roughly AUD 320,000). This is a hard constraint with financial consequences and must be surfaced to any user considering it, not just treated as essay framing.
- **Ordinary RTP** scholarships are administered by individual universities with their own selection processes, centred on research readiness, a supervisor, and a feasible project.

Write these as two genres. An Awards statement foregrounds development impact, leadership, and reintegration; an RTP application foregrounds research preparation and project fit.

### Hong Kong — HKPFS publishes its rubric

The Hong Kong PhD Fellowship Scheme (RGC/UGC) names **four yardsticks**: academic excellence (stated as the prime consideration), research ability/potential, communication and interpersonal skills, and leadership abilities. Shortlisted applicants are assessed by one of two panels (Sciences/Medicine/Engineering/Technology, or Humanities/Social Sciences/Business).

A published four-part rubric lets you allocate evidence directly: each criterion should have identifiable support somewhere in the package. Note that communication and leadership are scored here in a way most departmental PhD applications do not score them.

### Named-project vs department-first applications

Where the application targets a **specific funded project or advertised position** (much of Europe, MSCA/EURAXESS, UK studentships, Australian projects with a named supervisor), the statement argues fit to *that project* and shows the description was read. Where it targets a **department or cohort** (typical US admissions), it argues research direction and fit to several plausible advisors, since the advisor is not yet fixed.

### Application architecture varies independently of everything else

- A separate **research proposal** may carry the research content; where required, the statement should not duplicate it. Tsinghua, for example, requires international PhD applicants to submit research experience or a research plan **in addition to** the personal statement. That supports "research-plan specificity matters for this application" — it supports nothing about Chinese cultural preferences.
- Some European calls expect an actual **letter** — salutation, addressed to a named committee, signed — rather than a titled essay. Easy to verify from the call, and visible immediately if wrong.
- Mission-driven scholarships attach a **scored** dimension to post-degree plans and home-country contribution. Where it applies it needs concrete content, not sentiment.
- **Fellowship-specific criteria are not national criteria.** NSF's GRFP requires both written statements to address Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts under explicit headings — but that is a GRFP requirement, not evidence that "US committees value broader impact," and GRFP eligibility is restricted to U.S. citizens, nationals, and permanent residents. Scope such criteria to the programme that publishes them.

### Master's-level statements

Programme and curriculum choice, the bridge from prior study, and post-degree plans carry the document. Do not push an applicant to invent a research proposal the call did not ask for.

## Register claims are weakly sourced — do not assert them

Claims about how *personal*, *narrative*, or *formal* a statement should be by region are **widely repeated folklore**, and sources contradict each other on direction. They are hypotheses to test against the prompt, never rules to state to a user.

Reliable substitute: **read the prompt's own verbs.** "Why are you motivated to apply for this position" asks a different question from "describe your research background and goals." Answer the question actually asked, in the length actually allowed.

## Self-presentation and credit

Applicants most often understate their own contribution — "we developed," "I was involved in" — for work they personally did. This is a **precision** problem before it is a cultural one: a reader cannot award credit the document does not attribute.

Calibrate toward accuracy in both directions. Never inflate; never leave real work unattributed. Where stating a contribution feels uncomfortable, ask what specifically the user did — the discomfort usually locates the exact sentence, and the fix is factual specificity, not louder language. Do not coach a persona, and do not make claims about what people from any country are like.

## Language

Write in the language the call specifies. A statement in a second language should be read by a fluent speaker — committees distinguish awkward phrasing (forgivable) from an argument they cannot follow (not).

---

## Volatility

Prompts, word limits, required-document sets, eligibility, selection criteria, and return-home or residency conditions are **set per programme and per cycle** and carry real legal and financial consequences. Verify against the live official call. Nothing here overrides the call in front of the user.
